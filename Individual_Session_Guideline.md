# Individual Study Session

Each study session is one step within the general-topic learning cycle.

### Step 1: Define the session objective

Before the session, state one main question that the session must answer.

Also specify:

- required prerequisite knowledge;
- assigned material;
- expected preparation;
- expected session output.

The objective should focus on understanding a concept, mechanism, method, experiment, or limitation.

### Step 2: Prepare individually

Each member should study the assigned material before the meeting.

Preparation should include:

- identifying the problem being addressed;
- recording unfamiliar concepts;
- reconstructing the main argument or method;
- noting assumptions;
- identifying unclear or unsupported points;
- preparing questions.

When a paper is assigned, members should read it as a technical argument, not only as a source of conclusions.

### Step 3: Reconstruct the material together

During the session, establish a common understanding of:

- the problem;
- necessary background concepts;
- assumptions;
- method or reasoning process;
- inputs and outputs;
- training, inference, or control procedure;
- evaluation approach.

Members should explain these elements in their own words and reconstruct important equations, diagrams, or procedures when needed.

### Step 4: Examine the evidence

Discuss:

- what was actually tested;
- whether the evaluation matches the stated objective;
- which conclusions are supported;
- which conclusions depend on assumptions;
- what information is missing;
- what alternative explanation could produce similar results.

The session should separate demonstrated results from interpretation and speculation.

### Step 5: Implement or inspect experimentally

When necessary and feasible, complete a practical task such as:

- implementing the central mechanism;
- reproducing a reduced experiment;
- testing a simplified example;
- modifying one component;
- comparing against a basic alternative;
- inspecting an existing implementation;
- analyzing outputs or failure cases.

The task should answer a specific question. Implementation should not be performed only to demonstrate that code can run.

When implementation is not feasible, replace it with a detailed procedural reconstruction, numerical example, experiment design, or systematic analysis.

### Step 6: Record structured notes

The final session notes should contain:

1. **Session question**
2. **Required foundations**
3. **Main concepts**
4. **Method or reasoning**
5. **Important assumptions**
6. **Evidence and evaluation**
7. **Implementation or practical findings**
8. **Limitations and failure modes**
9. **Connections to the general topic**
10. **Unresolved questions and next steps**

Notes should clearly distinguish between:

- information from the studied material;
- the club’s interpretation;
- experimental observations;
- proposed explanations.

### Step 7: Verify the outcome

End the session by checking whether members can:

- answer the session question;
- explain the central mechanism;
- identify the main assumptions;
- describe the evidence;
- state at least one limitation;
- connect the session to previous and future sessions.

Any point that cannot be explained clearly should be recorded as unfinished work for the next session.
