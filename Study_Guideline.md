# Research Club Study Guideline

## 1. General Topic

Each general topic should be treated as a structured learning cycle made up of multiple individual study sessions.

### Step 1: Define the scope

State:

- what the topic covers;
- why it matters for physical AI;
- what level of understanding the club aims to reach;
- what is outside the scope.

The scope should be broad enough to build connected understanding but narrow enough to complete within a defined study cycle.

### Step 2: Identify required foundations

List the concepts that members must understand before studying the main topic.

Include, where relevant:

- mathematics;
- probability and statistics;
- optimization;
- machine-learning concepts;
- robotics, control, or physical-system concepts;
- software and experimental tools.

Do not assume that familiarity with terminology means genuine understanding. Missing foundations should be studied first or introduced before they are needed.

### Step 3: Build a concept map

Divide the topic into:

1. foundational concepts;
2. central methods and approaches;
3. practical implementation issues;
4. evaluation methods;
5. limitations and open problems;
6. connections to other areas of physical AI.

Arrange these elements in dependency order so that later sessions build on earlier ones.

### Step 4: Define learning outcomes

For the topic, specify what members should eventually be able to:

- explain;
- derive or reconstruct;
- implement or inspect;
- evaluate;
- compare;
- apply;
- question.

Learning outcomes should describe demonstrated competence rather than material that has merely been covered.

### Step 5: Plan the study sessions

Divide the topic into individual sessions.

Each session should address one coherent question or mechanism. The sequence should normally progress from foundations to methods, then to implementation, evaluation, and synthesis.

Implementation-focused sessions should be included when implementation is necessary for understanding and feasible with available resources.

### Step 6: Maintain a shared topic record

After every session, update one shared record containing:

- established concepts;
- important definitions and assumptions;
- mathematical or architectural relationships;
- implementation findings;
- unresolved questions;
- connections between sessions.

The record should represent the club’s current understanding of the topic, not a collection of disconnected summaries.

### Step 7: Conclude the topic

At the end of the cycle, review whether the learning outcomes were achieved.

Members should:

- reconstruct the main ideas without relying on source wording;
- explain how the concepts fit together;
- identify important assumptions and limitations;
- summarize implementation and evaluation lessons;
- identify remaining knowledge gaps;
- formulate possible research questions.

Unresolved gaps should be assigned to future study rather than hidden by a general summary.

## 2. Individual Study Session

Each study session is one step within the general-topic learning cycle.

### Step 1: Define the session objective

Before the session, state one main question that the session must answer.

Also specify:

- required prerequisite knowledge;
- assigned material;
- expected preparation;
- expected session output.

The objective should focus on understanding a concept, mechanism, method, experiment, or limitation.

### Step 2: Prepare individually

Each member should study the assigned material before the meeting.

Preparation should include:

- identifying the problem being addressed;
- recording unfamiliar concepts;
- reconstructing the main argument or method;
- noting assumptions;
- identifying unclear or unsupported points;
- preparing questions.

When a paper is assigned, members should read it as a technical argument, not only as a source of conclusions.

### Step 3: Reconstruct the material together

During the session, establish a common understanding of:

- the problem;
- necessary background concepts;
- assumptions;
- method or reasoning process;
- inputs and outputs;
- training, inference, or control procedure;
- evaluation approach.

Members should explain these elements in their own words and reconstruct important equations, diagrams, or procedures when needed.

### Step 4: Examine the evidence

Discuss:

- what was actually tested;
- whether the evaluation matches the stated objective;
- which conclusions are supported;
- which conclusions depend on assumptions;
- what information is missing;
- what alternative explanation could produce similar results.

The session should separate demonstrated results from interpretation and speculation.

### Step 5: Implement or inspect experimentally

When necessary and feasible, complete a practical task such as:

- implementing the central mechanism;
- reproducing a reduced experiment;
- testing a simplified example;
- modifying one component;
- comparing against a basic alternative;
- inspecting an existing implementation;
- analyzing outputs or failure cases.

The task should answer a specific question. Implementation should not be performed only to demonstrate that code can run.

When implementation is not feasible, replace it with a detailed procedural reconstruction, numerical example, experiment design, or systematic analysis.

### Step 6: Record structured notes

The final session notes should contain:

1. **Session question**
2. **Required foundations**
3. **Main concepts**
4. **Method or reasoning**
5. **Important assumptions**
6. **Evidence and evaluation**
7. **Implementation or practical findings**
8. **Limitations and failure modes**
9. **Connections to the general topic**
10. **Unresolved questions and next steps**

Notes should clearly distinguish between:

- information from the studied material;
- the club’s interpretation;
- experimental observations;
- proposed explanations.

### Step 7: Verify the outcome

End the session by checking whether members can:

- answer the session question;
- explain the central mechanism;
- identify the main assumptions;
- describe the evidence;
- state at least one limitation;
- connect the session to previous and future sessions.

Any point that cannot be explained clearly should be recorded as unfinished work for the next session.
